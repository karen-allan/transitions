RULES FOR REPRESENTATIVES
What a Representative may do:
  • The authority of a representative appointed under this representation agreement includes the power to give or refuse consent to health care necessary to preserve life.
What a Representative can not do:
  • give or refuse consent on your behalf to any health care prescribed under the Health Care Act
  • make arrangements for the temporary care and education of your minor children or other dependents.
  • interfere with your religious practices.
  • may not be authorized to refuse consent to those matters in relation to the Mental Health Act.
  • must not consent to sterilization for non-therapeutic purposes.
  • must not make or change your will.

PRIMARY REPRESENTATIVE:
This form provides for the naming of one Primary Representative and one Alternate Representative. The Representation Agreement Act sets out who may be named as a representative. They must:
  • be 19 years of age or older
  • not be an individual who provides personal or health care services to you for compensation
  • not be an employee of a facility in which you reside and receive personal or health care services (unless the employee is your child, parent or spouse)

INFORMATION FOR WITNESSES
(a) The following persons may not be a witness:
  • A person named in the representation agreement as a representative.
  • A spouse, child or parent of a person named in the representation agreement as a representative.
  • An employee or agent of a person named in the representation agreement as a representative, unless the person is a lawyer, a Notary Public of B.C., or the Public Guardian and Trustee of B.C.
  • A person who is under 19 years of age.
  • A person who does not understand the communication used by the adult unless the person receives interpretive assistance.
Only one witness is required if the witness is a lawyer or a member in good standing of the Society of Notaries Public of B.C.
