Let's say you are in an accident or suffer a critical illness and end up in Emergency and are incapable of answering for yourself. When you arrive at the hospital - a health care team member must contact your next-of-kin in order to find an advocate for you. This is the critical moment where what you have done or not done in the past to plan for your own care will dictate what happens next. Who do you trust with your life?

Everyone no matter their age should create an advance care plan to go along with their will and other legal documents but this is specifically important for those who have any of the chronic health conditions.

It's Time to Plan

If you have these or any other serious, chronic condition or have been told that you have some kind of time limit to your life, it is very important to put an advance care plan together for your family to follow. In addition to selecting the person or persons who will Advocate for you in the event of an emergency, an advance care plan allows you to state specific treatments and procedures that you may want to say yes or no to.

The plan can be as simple or as detailed as you like and can include Do Not Resuscitate, No CPR orders, or plans for an EDITH (Expected Death in the Home).

Why Write an Advance Directive?

  • You want to be in charge of your own health, life and death.
  • Your realize that doing so now could lead to less family conflicts later.
  • You trust your advocates but want to lessen the burden of them having to make difficult decisions.
  • You want to eliminate confusion for your family.

By creating an advance care directive, you can state specifically which treatments and procedures you want or do not want. It provides another vehicle to letting the health care providers know what your wishes are. Your directive will be followed by the health care providers as it pertains directly to the situation at hand.

An advance care plan becomes the final say - no matter what your advocates promote or try to deny you. If you are concerned that your wishes may not be followed by your advocate, you will need to state in the advance directive that your health care providers may act in accordance with your instructions without the consent of your representative. You can even name the procedures you would like attempted as well as the treatments you would not want.

Let your Needs be Known

If you don't have an advance directive or your advance directive doesn't include a proposed health care treatment then your advocate either appointed as your Formal Substitute Decision Maker or your chosen legal representative(s) will be asked to make these decisions.

This means for example, if you don’t want to be intubated and it says so in your directive you will not be. If your directive doesn’t mention being intubated then the decision will be made by your advocate. If you don’t have an advocate, the decision will be made by the health care team.

You can ask your doctor to add a copy of your directive to your medical records. Keep a copy where it can be easily found in an emergency, and let your family and advocates know this location. Do not store your Advance Directive in a safety deposit box or in any locked box which could prove difficult for your family to open.
