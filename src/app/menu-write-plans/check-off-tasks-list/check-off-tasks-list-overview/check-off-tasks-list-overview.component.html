Step 1: Get a Storage Space for your Papers:

Purchase a hard covered safe, a safety deposit box or even purchase a small storage unit to keep all your papers in one place, safe from weather, floods, fire etc.

Step 2: Do the Financial Stuff

Just like doing your taxes, nobody likes to do the sorting of finances but it is a chore that every responsible Canadian should do. Leaving it for your kids or other family to work out will undoubtedly cause a lot of needless stress and tension. Put yourself in the shoes of your Executor and imagine all of the information that they will need. Please note that we are focused on the health care aspects of dying so there can be many financial decisions and actions needed to be made. You will definitely want to put together a Will either through a lawyer or notary public or by purchasing a Will kit to do yourself. Here are a few basic things to consider:

  • The contact information for your Lawyer or Notary.
  • Whether you have a will and the location of your Will.
  • The company that provides your Life Insurance.
  • Any trusts you have set up.
  • Any long term disability, critical illness or long term care insurance.
  • The person and contact information for the person you have chosen to be your Power of Attorney.
  • The location and email addresses of any banks or investment companies you deal with.
  • Your bank account numbers and any other password information needed to obtain your money.
  • Organ donor permission form (sign up at transplant.bc.ca).
  • If you have a cemetery plot paid for, the location of it.
  • The notes detailing any paid for funeral expenses.
  • The contact information for all relevant people who need to be informed.
